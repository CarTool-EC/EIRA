Social Participation ()
Definition: Social participation refers to Promoting Civic Participation and Transparency of Government Decision-Making Processes covering policies concerning ICT. The usage of digital environments can promote new kinds of sociability, support, and usability or revive certainly lost facets of social ties.The construction of more effective public services, as well as new kinds of communication and interaction between citizens and the various levels of government, can all be facilitated by the use of digital tools. Even more public involvement in various levels of local management processes is possible with their use. These fresh working alliances also contribute to the legitimacy and increased transparency of public activity. Some examples of social participation are:• E-democracy• E-participation• Government-to-citizen (G2C)• Citizen-to-government (C2G)• Citizen-to-citizen (C2C)• Open governance partnership• Grass-root activism and social networking• Political campaigns• Online media• I-voting• Mechanisms: e-petitions, online consultation and crowd-sourcing platforms etc. The implications of social participation are related to the creation and implementation of policies that include as the backbone, the citizens and businesses. Additionally, to policies that govern, it is relevant that public administrations implement tools that foster, enable and ensure stakeholders participation. dct:titleSocial Participation
dct:description(Statement) Social participation fosters and ensures that all stakeholders, including users, service providers, technology partners, and professionals, are represented and engaged in the design, creation, and maintenance of digital public services. This approach promotes civic participation and transparency in government decision-making processes, particularly concerning ICT policies.
dct:description(Rationale) The integration of Social participation and public-private governance provides significant benefits for the development and maintenance of digital public services. A sound governance model is essential for creating a level playing field where all stakeholders feel represented and engaged.This includes users (individuals and organisations), service providers, and technology partners. By involving all relevant parties, public administrations can ensure that the solutions developed are comprehensive, effective, and aligned with the needs and expectations of all stakeholders.The approach of this principle fosters collaboration, innovation, and trust, which are crucial for the successful implementation and sustainability of digital public services. It can help to ease the creation of more effective public services, communication and interaction between citizens and various levels of government.
dct:description(Implications) Systems must be designed to support collaborative governance, with tools and platforms that facilitate stakeholder engagement and participation. Public administrations must adopt policies and frameworks that promote the active involvement of all stakeholders, ensuring that their needs and perspectives are considered in the design, creation, and maintenance of digital public services. Additionally, policies should be implemented to ensure that citizens and businesses are at the core of these initiatives, fostering a culture of transparency and accountability.
